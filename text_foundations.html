I have a fuzzy goal and no particular inclination right now. I think my case is a bit worse than the average person's because I have had a clear goal and a soul-igniting dream.

Couple this with my need for immediate feedback, need to get a job out of college soon, and many paths with no concrete answer as to whether it is apt for my character

However, there are some points of glimmering light. I know there is no silver bullet - the best right path is never taken by deliberately walking the optimal path. In fact, the best path is the best path because it wasn't the best. As a coach, I can give students the shortcut I learned from trial-and-error, but it is the trial-and-error that gave me the depth of understanding.

Sure, 100% trial-and-error is never the answer, but 80% trial-error and 20% outside learning is much better. Again, don't ever let the 20% (trying to optimize for the right answer) become more than 20%.

Another thing is local optimization. The best choices are what's in front of you. They can be assessed (people can't forecast) and taken up on easily. Enough local optimization becomes a career of happy accidents. We also don;t know what we want, and we also don't know what we don't know! Seems like being a person means knowing you know nothing, including what I want. Operating in uncertainty is the only certainty.

Guides to operate in uncertainty: make intuitive estimations (not calculations), know you don't know what you want, you don't know what you don't know, exploration + sticking with a choice is always better than pure optimization in either, stay in the game, don't optimize for metas, keep surface area open to capture tail events, understand tradeoffs

The best action is action! The only time I felt like I wasted my time was when I never took action. Time spent thinking is time not spent taking action. Don't get complex, problems are solved with two variables at most; Don;t think greater than that (stick to bare simple basics. Any deeper action like what I mentioned in the above paragraph is applicable but shouldn't be the focus)

Guiding Myself

the Best Path

However, there are some points of glimmering light. I know there is no silver bullet - the best right path is never taken by deliberately walking the optimal path. In fact, the best path is the best path because it wasn't the best. As a coach, I can give students the shortcut I learned from trial-and-error, but it is the trial-and-error that gave me the depth of understanding.

The Best Path, pt.2

Sure, 100% trial-and-error is never the answer, but 80% trial-error and 20% outside learning is much better. Again, don't ever let the 20% (trying to optimize for the right answer) become more than 20%.

Local Optimization, Not knowing what we want

Another thing is local optimization. The best choices are what's in front of you. They can be assessed (people can't forecast) and taken up on easily. Enough local optimization becomes a career of happy accidents. We also don't know what we want, and we also don't know what we don't know! Seems like being human is a lot of not knowing. Operating in uncertainty is the only certainty.

Overdone Maxims for Uncertainty

Guides to operate in uncertainty: make intuitive estimations (not calculations), know you don't know what you want, you don't know what you don't know, exploration + sticking with a choice is always better than pure optimization in either, stay in the game, don't optimize for metas, keep surface area open to capture tail events, understand tradeoffs

Simply - what to take away

The best action is action! The only time I felt like I wasted my time was when I never took action. Time spent thinking is time not spent taking action. Problems are solved with two variables at most, don't think beyond that (maxims above are applicable, but use them as estimations, not a list of concrete truths to abosulutely follow for every decision) Yes they may all be true but viewing them as benchmarks to meet instead of guideposts makes them like Goodhart's Law. The more I optimize for them, the less effective they actually become. It becomes a situation where estimating is better because it is like trying to manually fill a jar with solid objects instead of just filling with water (adaptive, flexible). In theory, solids can fit the jar the same as water, but it is not practical at all.

Summarizing Points

Inaction is the worst action. Know how to operate in uncertainty. Remember, most problems are solved in two variables (variables interfere w others). Use estimations to guide you, not hard-set rules.
